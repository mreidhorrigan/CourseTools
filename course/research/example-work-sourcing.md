# Example-work sourcing and grading research

## Purpose

Example work can test whether rubrics recognize strong design and can help students interpret assignment requirements. A useful example needs enough context to show what it demonstrates and what remains inapplicable.

## Selection rules

1. Prefer creator-, publisher-, or instructor-hosted sources.
2. Record the exact assignment or production context, creator, stable URL, version or access date, licence, and permitted use.
3. Redistribute a transcript, project file, or student submission only when its licence or written permission allows redistribution. Public readability alone is insufficient.
4. Link to copyrighted work and provide an original analytical guide when redistribution is unclear. Keep any permitted local research copy outside public course packages unless student access is authorized.
5. Remove student identifiers and private feedback. Do not reuse student work as a grading benchmark without consent and an appropriate retention basis.
6. Separate portable design criteria from course-specific compliance criteria. A professional game or actual-play episode cannot demonstrate a hypothetical student’s individual contribution, required course chatlog, or submission packaging.
7. Record limitations and have a human review every model-produced benchmark grade.

## Candidate actual-play evidence

Critical Role episodes provide professionally produced actual play with public video captions. A focused scene may demonstrate consequential choice, facilitation, listener orientation, editing, speaker identification, and meaningful non-speech information. Before any course use, identify the official episode and timestamp, confirm the current caption/transcript source and terms, and use a link plus an original observation worksheet unless redistribution permission is explicit. A complete commercial transcript must not be bundled merely because it can be downloaded.

## Candidate coursework evidence

Search for instructor-curated game-design courses that publish the assignment prompt, selected student output, rationale for selection, and reuse terms together. Prefer work released under a Creative Commons licence or accompanied by written permission. Preserve a source manifest and file hashes for every downloaded artifact. Treat professional games and instructor-curated student examples as different benchmark classes.
