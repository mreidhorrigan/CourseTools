# Game studies roadmap: research record

The student-facing roadmap is maintained in `course/content/pages/start-here.html`.
It is an orientation map rather than a claim to a settled canon. Canon formation in
game studies has often excluded board games, marginalized players, noncommercial
production, and scholarship outside English-language digital-game studies.

## Editorial principles

- Show intellectual lineages and disagreements, including play theory, formalism,
  narratology, ludology, design research, ethnography, history, political economy,
  and critical cultural studies.
- Mark assigned works by week and describe unassigned works as orientation.
- Prefer stable DOI, journal, author, university-repository, and scholarly-society
  links. Open access affects link choice; it does not determine canonicity.
- Present the narratology–ludology debate as a formative historical episode. Markku
  Eskelinen's 2001 “The Gaming Situation” is a primary document in that episode;
  later work substantially complicated its binary.
- Treat glossary definitions as entry points into arguments whose meanings vary by
  author and period.
