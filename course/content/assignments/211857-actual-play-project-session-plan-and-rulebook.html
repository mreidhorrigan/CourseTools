Submit one PDF that another group could use to understand the planned game, run the session responsibly, record it, and determine whether the project is ready for production. Organize the PDF under the headings below. The plan is graded for concrete, usable decisions; naming a topic without specifying how it will work does not demonstrate a requirement.

Choose an existing system

Select one of the following rules systems or playkits. The recorded actual-play episode should function like an improvised television episode: players make consequential choices in character, while facilitation, pacing, scene framing, performance, recording, and editing make those choices intelligible to an audience.

  • Dungeons & Dragons System Reference Document 5.2.1
  • Cairn Barebones Edition
  • Fate Accelerated
  • Basic Fantasy Role-Playing Game
  • Ironsworn: Starforged playkit
  • Blades in the Dark downloads

Apply the selected system's existing procedures and materials. A team may extend or adapt rules only when the applicable licence or explicit permission allows that use. Credit the system and identify the licence or permission basis. Distinguish published rules from team-created additions. Do not import protected settings, characters, artwork, music, or trademarks merely because a rules document is available.

Required sections

  1. Selected system, rules, and playable situation. Identify the selected system and the version or dated source used. State the setting or context, participant roles and objectives, initial conditions, turn or action procedure, resolution rules, and end condition. Identify which procedures are applied unchanged and which, if any, are team extensions. Identify at least one consequential player choice: a choice whose outcome changes the state of play. The rulebook should be detailed enough for the planned participants to use during the dry run.
  2. Consent and safety. Identify foreseeable content or participation risks, the pre-session consent process, lines or boundaries, the in-session pause/change/leave procedure, and the post-session check-in. Explain how a participant can withdraw permission for material involving them before submission.
  3. Production workflow and responsibilities. Specify the primary and backup recording methods, file formats and naming, storage and backup, editing workflow, and final export. List each team member's production and session responsibilities and the date by which each action must be completed.
  4. Accessibility and listener access. Identify barriers expected in play or in the edited episode and the concrete features that will address them. Include the transcript or caption workflow and at least one feature that helps a listener follow participants, rules, choices, or scene changes.
  5. Dry-run test. Write at least one testable question about agency, rules, facilitation, timing, recording, or listener comprehension. State what evidence will answer it, when the dry run will occur, and which revisions or decisions may follow.
  6. Schedule and definition of done. List production milestones and a checklist of observable conditions that must be true before recording and before final submission.

AI appendix

If AI was used for this milestone, append the complete relevant chatlogs after the plan and label them AI appendix. Do not include passwords, private participant information, or unrelated conversations. If AI was not used, state “No AI used for this milestone.”

Before submitting

  • Open the final PDF and confirm that every page is readable.
  • Confirm that all six required sections are present and internally consistent.
  • Confirm that responsibilities, dates, backup recording, and the dry-run evidence are specific.
  • Remove credentials and private information that the instructional team does not need.
