Back to syllabus criteria

Submit exactly two files: one MP4 gameplay episode and one ODT companion document. A filename, checklist, or unsupported claim is not evidence: the two files must contain enough material for the instructional team to evaluate every rubric criterion.

Required files

  1. Gameplay episode (MP4). Include one 10–15 minute video showing a playable session using one approved existing system. Treat the episode as an improvised television episode: the play remains genuinely responsive to player decisions, while scene framing, pacing, facilitation, performance, recording, and editing make it coherent for an audience. The episode must make at least one consequential player choice and its outcome understandable. Participants, important rules, choices, and scene changes should remain intelligible to someone who did not attend the session.
  2. Companion document (ODT). Begin with an accurate, timestamped transcript of the submitted episode. Label every speaker consistently and describe meaningful non-speech information needed to follow play. Add a rubric evidence map after the transcript. Give each rubric criterion its own labelled entry. For evidence visible or audible in the episode, identify the exact timestamp or time range, describe the event, and explain briefly how the event demonstrates the criterion. For documentary evidence that does not occur in the episode, identify the relevant ODT heading instead of inventing a timestamp. The evidence map helps the marker locate evidence; it does not replace the evidence itself.
  3. Final rules and session materials in the ODT. Identify the selected system and include the final rulebook or a precise reference to the published rules used, along with every team-created material needed to understand or reproduce the submitted session. Mark published procedures and team extensions clearly. Embed or reproduce character sheets, scenario prompts, maps, handouts, reference cards, random tables, or safety materials only when they apply to the project.
  4. Consent and permissions record in the ODT. Include a brief record confirming that each recorded participant agreed to recording and course submission, knew how to pause or withdraw, and approved the submitted use. A dated team record of confirmation is sufficient. Do not include signatures, private boundaries, accommodation details, identification documents, or information the instructional team does not need.
  5. Credits and licences in the ODT. Identify the creator, title, version, source link, and licence or permission basis for the selected rules system and for music, sound, images, fonts, software, and other third-party material. State when the team created an item. If rules were extended, identify the licence provision or explicit permission that authorizes adaptation and provide the required attribution.
  6. Testing and revision record in the ODT. Document at least one dry run or test: date, participants or roles, question tested, evidence observed, and problems found. Identify at least one resulting revision, with a before/after description and a reason. If the team retained a tested design, explain what evidence supported that decision.
  7. Individual contribution and analysis in the ODT. Include one clearly labelled entry from each student. Each entry identifies concrete contributions and analyzes how at least one decision or contribution affected player agency, facilitation, listener understanding, accessibility, or the finished episode.
  8. AI appendix in the ODT, when applicable. If AI was used, include the complete relevant chatlogs and identify the project files or decisions they informed. Do not include passwords, private participant information, or unrelated conversations. No appendix is required when AI was not used.

Before submitting

  • Open the MP4 and ODT on a different device or user account.
  • Confirm that the episode duration is 10–15 minutes and that the timestamped transcript matches it.
  • Check every rubric-evidence-map entry against the rubric and the cited timestamp or ODT heading.
  • Confirm that the package demonstrates player agency, responsible facilitation, listener intelligibility, access, and evidence-based revision.
  • Remove credentials, unnecessary personal information, and private consent boundaries.
