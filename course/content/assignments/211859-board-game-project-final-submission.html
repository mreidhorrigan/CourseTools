Submit one ZIP that allows an unfamiliar group to reconstruct, learn, and assess the game without the designers present. One team member may upload the ZIP; every member must check the submitted file.

Playable package

  • Rules PDF: state audience, player count, duration, goal, component inventory, setup, turn or round sequence, legal actions, resolution procedures, end condition, and tie handling. Include examples or diagrams where prose alone is likely to be ambiguous.
  • Printable-components PDF: include every board, card, tile, track, player aid, or marker that can reasonably be printed.
  • Non-printable-piece sheet: identify required dice, pawns, cubes, timers, or other pieces and provide an accessible simulation or facsimile for each. A simulation reproduces the piece’s game function; a facsimile is a labelled substitute with equivalent states or values.
  • Gameplay video: show setup, a representative turn or round, consequential choices, feedback from the system, and an end or scoring procedure.

Testing and production evidence

  • Playtest record: include at least one blind or low-intervention rules test with unfamiliar players. Record date, player count, duration, recurring questions, observed choices, stalled states, score or outcome range, and other evidence relevant to the design.
  • Balance and revision record: state each investigated concern, the evidence used, what changed, and what happened afterward. Balance means that intended choices or strategies remain meaningfully viable for the stated audience and experience goal; it does not require equal outcomes in every session.
  • Credits: identify creators, sources, licences, and modifications for third-party text and assets. State which work is original.
  • Individual contribution evidence: for each member, list concrete contributions and analyze one effect on the game’s rules, player experience, testing, or production.
  • AI appendix, when applicable: include complete relevant chatlogs when AI contributed to the submitted work. Remove credentials and private third-party information. No appendix is required when AI was not used.

Evaluation focus

The game must present meaningful choices, legible feedback, and coherent interactions among its rules and components. Visual finish can improve usability, but presentation alone does not establish playability, balance, or revision. Markers use the submitted artifact and evidence; claimed effort and raw iteration counts do not substitute for demonstrated results.
