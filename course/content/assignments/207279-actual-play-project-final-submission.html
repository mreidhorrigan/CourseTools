Actual-Play Project: Final Submission

Submit one ZIP containing the complete project package. A filename or checklist alone is not evidence: each required file must contain enough material for the instructional team to evaluate the corresponding rubric criterion.

Required ZIP contents

  1. Gameplay episode. Include one 10–15 minute audio or video file showing a playable session. The episode must make at least one consequential player choice and its outcome understandable. Edit and organize the episode for a listener or viewer who did not attend the session; participants, important rules, choices, and scene changes should remain intelligible.
  2. Transcript or captions. Include an accurate transcript or caption file for the submitted episode. Identify speakers consistently and represent meaningful non-speech information needed to follow play.
  3. Final rules and session materials. Include the final rulebook and every material needed to understand or reproduce the submitted session, such as character sheets, scenario prompts, maps, handouts, reference cards, random tables, or safety materials. Include only items that apply to this project.
  4. Consent and permissions record. Include a brief record confirming that each recorded participant agreed to recording and course submission, knew how to pause or withdraw, and approved the submitted use. A signed form, dated digital confirmation, or dated team record of verbal confirmation is acceptable. Do not include private boundaries, accommodation details, identification documents, or information the instructional team does not need.
  5. Credits and licences. Identify the creator and licence or permission basis for music, sound, images, rules, systems, fonts, software, and other third-party material. State when the team created an item. Include links where relevant.
  6. Testing and revision record. Document at least one dry run or test: date, participants or roles, question tested, evidence observed, and problems found. Identify at least one resulting revision, with a before/after description and a reason. If the team retained a tested design, explain what evidence supported that decision.
  7. Individual contribution and analysis. Include one clearly labelled entry from each student. Each entry identifies concrete contributions and analyzes how at least one decision or contribution affected player agency, facilitation, listener understanding, accessibility, or the finished episode.
  8. AI appendix. If AI was used, include the complete relevant chatlogs and identify the project files or decisions they informed. Do not include passwords, private participant information, or unrelated conversations. If AI was not used, include a text file stating “No AI used for this project.”

Before submitting

  • Extract the ZIP into an empty folder and open every required file.
  • Confirm that the episode duration is 10–15 minutes and that its transcript or captions match it.
  • Confirm that the package demonstrates player agency, responsible facilitation, listener intelligibility, access, and evidence-based revision.
  • Remove credentials, unnecessary personal information, and private consent boundaries.
